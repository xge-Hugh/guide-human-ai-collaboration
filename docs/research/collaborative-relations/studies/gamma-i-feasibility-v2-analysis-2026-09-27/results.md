# γ-I v2 frozen feasibility result — 2026-09-27

**Decision:** The frozen v2 packet and audit procedure were executable: 27 valid fresh recipient contexts, three per fixture × condition cell, produced bounded audit selections and final responses. The result supports a **mechanics-feasibility finding**, not a treatment-effect or A3/A3a claim update. Do not take this packet/scorer combination directly into a larger confirmatory γ-I run. D contains extra consequence linkage and verification guidance, and the scorer repeatedly misclassifies the C13 reviewer-only future holdout as a current required item. A larger study needs a prospective packet design that isolates that guidance and a validated scoring implementation; this run's frozen inputs and judgments remain unchanged.

The table counts *scorer judgments* of full recognition of the two required current-decision items: three recipient runs × two scorer contexts × two items = 12 judgments per cell. The other judgments were partial or missed; the [descriptive data](descriptive-summary.json) retains item-level counts. The 3 recipients per cell are the experimental units, so these counts are not 12 independent replications.

At least one fixture avoids ceiling performance across conditions, but S01 and SH2 reach ceiling under both T and D, so they cannot discriminate those two settlement forms here. C13 is the sole corpus-derived case and remains separate from the synthetic apparatus checks. The C13 D/T difference of one judgment is too small and too confounded to identify fact-selection benefit. F recipients often chose a justified inspect or verification action despite only partial initial recognition; missing specifics did not automatically mean unjustified uncertainty. The scorer pairs rated 25/27 initial next actions identically and 50/54 initial required-item judgments identically.

The frozen [pre-exposure adjudication](../gamma-i-feasibility-v2-run-2026-09-27-03/preflight/interpretation-adjudication.md) retained the S01 review's warning that D includes guidance beyond T, while allowing this mechanics run under the PR #41 treatment-leakage rule. The adjudicator was also the runner implementer, not an independent second reviewer. Thus no D advantage here is evidence for fact selection alone, regardless of direction or size. The review found no answer-bearing index labels or audit-oracle/future-holdout leakage into recipient payloads; it does not remove the guidance confound.

## Post-index selection and post-payload judgment

All 27 recipients made recordable two-item selections from the common fixture index. C13 F selected R1/R5 in all three runs; C13 T always selected R1 plus R3 or R4; C13 D always selected R3 plus R1 or R2. S01 F and D always selected R4/R1; S01 T always selected R4 plus R1 or R5. SH2 F always selected R1 plus R3 or R4; SH2 T always selected R3 plus R1 or R2; SH2 D always selected R2/R3. These are choices after a new information surface and must not be credited to the initial settlement packet. The raw selection text, exact returned payloads, and scorer relevance assessments remain in the [run and score records](./).

After the selected payloads, full recognition was C13 F 2/12, T 6/12, D 10/12; S01 F/T/D 12/12 each; SH2 F 8/12, T 12/12, D 11/12. The index and payloads visibly changed the information available, especially for F. These final counts do not estimate an initial-packet treatment effect. The scorer pairs agreed on 51/54 final required-item judgments and 26/27 final action ratings. Agreement is reproducibility between two Qwen contexts, not independent proof that either is correct.

## Scorer validity and operational limits

One scorer response was complete but invalid JSON. Its original request and response are preserved under `scores/C13-F-1/scorer-2.*`; an independent `scorer-2-retry1` call with the same frozen grading prompt supplied the valid second judgment. Across 55 scorer responses, 54 valid parsed judgments were used; no raw judgment was edited. The scoring process interruption left four calls without responses; only those missing contexts and the malformed-response retry were invoked on resume.

The grader added the C13 `F1` future holdout to `d_now_plus` in 12 of 18 C13 score files (24 phase entries), despite the frozen bounded rubric and the explicit instruction that F1 is reviewer context, not a retrospective answer key. It also added `E4` or `E6` as extra plus items in three files each. The [descriptive analyzer](descriptive-summary.json) counts only the frozen required C13 `E2`/`E5` items and flags every extra; this is a reporting normalization, not a changed scoring rule. Some scorer fields varied in JSON shape, so the analyzer extracts plainly expressed categorical action ratings while preserving the original parsed and raw responses. Implementation-confidence reports used heterogeneous scales, so no cross-run confidence mean is reported. These defects limit the scorer's fitness for confirmatory use even though required-item agreement was high.

The nine settlement surfaces ranged from 37 to 168 words; initial recipient answers averaged 309–386 words by cell, and returned audit payloads averaged 59–224 words. These are coordination-exposure proxies, not cognitive-effort measures. The provider reported 81 recipient responses (three per run) and 55 scorer responses, about 386k and 1.26m total tokens respectively, including the invalid scorer response; calls were concurrent in scoring, so summed call durations are not wall-clock runtime. No high-confidence unsupported acceptance was established by the frozen scorer record. Two of 54 judgments per phase left that flag null, and the protocol set no numeric high-confidence threshold, so the absence of a flag is not a calibrated safety rate.

The [execution snapshot](execution-code/manifest.json), [recipient integrity result](final-integrity.json), [descriptive summary](descriptive-summary.json), preflight reviews, all raw model requests/responses, and excluded-attempt records preserve the audit trail. Five runner tests pass. The final integrity check found 27/27 valid recipients and 54 scorer artifacts; a scan of 621 study artifact files found no private settings values. No treatment packet, oracle, scoring rule, or claim interpretation was modified during execution.

The frozen feasibility criteria are met for rendering, recipient isolation, recordable audit requests, and avoiding a universal ceiling/floor collapse. The clean D-versus-T interpretation and scorer-validity criteria are not met for a larger study. The next study design should isolate consequence linkage/guidance prospectively, validate the scorer against the C13 holdout boundary, and add corpus-derived fixtures before any A3/A3a inference. This is a recommendation for future work, not a revision of this frozen run.
