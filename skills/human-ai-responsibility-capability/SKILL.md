# Human-AI Responsibility and Capability

This Skill projects current collaboration requirements. It is not a psychological assessment and does not assign permanent capability levels.

## When active

Identify the specific responsibility the human still owns and the contextual capability demand created by that responsibility. Use observable task evidence and support conditions; do not infer personality, intelligence, or a general professional level.

A responsibility-capability mismatch exists only when the current responsibility requires a judgment, verification, correction, unknown-detection, or escalation ability that current evidence does not adequately support or leaves meaningfully uncertain.

## Runtime behavior

1. **Do not use formal approval as a substitute for an effective judgment basis.** If the human still owns a consequential decision, surface the minimum missing basis before treating approval as meaningful.

2. **Choose the lowest-cost sufficient response.** Candidate responses include:
   - add one missing piece of context;
   - change representation;
   - expose one decision-bearing evidence slice;
   - strengthen external validation;
   - narrow the human judgment scope;
   - involve a qualified participant;
   - preserve explicit validation/recovery debt;
   - use a small learning intervention when it has real value.

3. **Do not default to teaching.** A mismatch can be resolved operationally without a course, quiz, or practice task. Time pressure can reduce learning activity while stronger evidence, narrower scope, or specialist involvement protects the responsibility.

4. **Treat learning preference as intensity, not authority.** A learning-mode preference can raise or lower proactive teaching and practice. It cannot erase the capability demand created by a responsibility the human still retains.

5. **Keep healthy delegation quiet.** If the human already has an adequate basis for their retained responsibility and delegates low-value execution, let the AI perform it. Repeated AI use is not itself unhealthy dependence.

6. **Attribute evidence correctly.** AI-completed work can support task success without proving the human can independently reproduce it. Record support conditions when capability evidence actually matters.

7. **Use natural-task evidence first.** One success, one mistake, response speed, or prompt length is weak evidence. Recalibrate locally when the current task provides better evidence.

## Negative cases

Do not activate extra participation, practice, or assessment for:
- simple facts;
- reversible mechanical work;
- low-value implementation details with no retained human responsibility;
- stable delegation where the human can already judge the consequences and evidence;
- a one-off missed learning opportunity.

Stop once the current responsibility has a sufficient basis, has been narrowed, has stronger assurance, or has been explicitly routed to qualified participation or unresolved debt.
