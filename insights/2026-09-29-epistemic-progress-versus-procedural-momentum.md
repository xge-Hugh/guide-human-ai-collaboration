# Epistemic progress versus procedural momentum

- **Date**: 2026-09-29
- **Status**: candidate project-governance method
- **Semantic type**: lightweight research-process insight; not a project norm, not a mandatory workflow
- **Source**: Experiment γ progression, especially the late realization that a technically disciplined AI-recipient replay had become weak evidence for a human-centered research question

## 1. Field signal

Experiment γ accumulated substantial procedural progress: protocol refinement, oracle freezing, source-review gates, audit-payload repair, runtime isolation, 27 AI-recipient runs, scorer contexts, and integrity/provenance records.

Most local next steps were individually reasonable. Yet the human collaborator eventually experienced a persistent mismatch: substantial effort, duration, and apparatus complexity had accumulated without a correspondingly useful change in understanding of the human phenomenon that motivated the study.

That dissatisfaction triggered a frame-level question:

> If the project is studying human understanding, judgment, and robustness, why is the primary controlled replay using AI recipients as the stand-in for humans?

The useful correction did not come from another local apparatus repair. It came from questioning the method's relationship to the target construct.

## 2. Candidate distinction

~~~text
procedural progress
= what has been designed, implemented, run, repaired, or documented

epistemic progress
= what important belief, uncertainty, boundary, prediction,
  or project decision has actually changed
~~~

The two can diverge. A project can become increasingly rigorous at executing a method while that method yields little additional target-relevant knowledge.

> **A technically executable next step is not sufficient evidence that it is the most valuable next step.**

## 3. Candidate trigger: epistemic-progress checkpoint

Do not add a mandatory questionnaire after every task. Trigger a frame-level check only when both are present:

~~~text
substantial accumulated activity / cost
+
weak or repeatedly non-discriminating epistemic yield
~~~

Possible signals include:

- several iterations mainly repair the apparatus rather than change the claim;
- the next step is clear operationally but its expected decision-relevant information is unclear;
- repeated results establish only feasibility while the research question concerns effectiveness or human cognition;
- human collaborators report that the work is becoming elaborate without producing useful understanding;
- alternative evidence routes have not been reconsidered for several cycles.

A human signal such as "this has gone on for a long time and I still do not think we learned anything important" is not automatically correct, but should count as high-value anomaly evidence that makes the current frame inspectable.

## 4. Minimal checkpoint

When triggered, ask only:

1. **Target fidelity** — are we actually observing the population or phenomenon the claim concerns?
2. **Discrimination** — what plausible result from the next step would materially change the current belief or decision?
3. **Epistemic yield** — what important uncertainty has the current method reduced so far?
4. **Alternative evidence** — is there existing research, naturalistic evidence, another population, or another method that could answer the question more directly or cheaply?

Possible outcomes: continue, amend the method, change the evidence route, park the question, or stop.

The checkpoint should not become a ritual where every continuation requires formal scoring.

## 5. Candidate human–AI asymmetry behind the signal

The γ episode suggests a possible research hypothesis:

- AI is often strong at local continuation, formalization, systematic elaboration, and repairing the currently represented method.
- The human owns the purpose of the inquiry and bears the lived opportunity cost of time, complexity, and low perceived value.
- A discrepancy between formal progress and experienced usefulness may therefore become salient to the human before it becomes salient to an AI that is successfully solving each local next step.

This should not be interpreted as "humans are inherently better at framing." The narrower candidate is:

> Purpose ownership plus lived opportunity-cost signals may provide a distinct route for detecting frame mismatch.

AI may compensate by explicitly comparing procedural momentum against expected epistemic yield when the mismatch becomes large.

## 6. Expected-epistemic-delta heuristic

A candidate safeguard for substantial research continuation:

> **Before committing to another expensive research step, be able to state the expected epistemic delta: what belief, distinction, uncertainty, boundary, or project decision could change if the step succeeds or fails.**

If the answer is only "we will have completed the next experiment / built the next apparatus / collected more runs," the continuation needs stronger justification.

This refines the existing decision-driven evidence-cycle insight by making one failure mode explicit: **method continuation can become locally coherent while globally low-yield**.

## 7. Boundary and falsification

This candidate would be weakened if repeated project episodes show that:

- the checkpoint mostly interrupts productive research before delayed evidence arrives;
- subjective low-yield signals are too noisy to improve method selection;
- ordinary decision-driven evidence routing already catches the same failures without an additional concept;
- AI-side epistemic-yield monitoring adds cost without changing decisions.

It gains value only if it repeatedly causes a substantive change in method, claim boundary, or evidence route before unnecessary procedural investment accumulates.

## 8. Relation to Experiment γ

The γ case is lineage, not proof of this method.

Its strongest current lesson is:

~~~text
successful apparatus execution
does not imply
successful inquiry into the target construct
~~~

The completed γ-I AI-recipient feasibility run remains useful for apparatus feasibility and AI-side reconstruction behavior. It should not be retrospectively re-labelled as evidence about human cognition.
